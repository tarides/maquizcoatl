# Experiment #12, entry 5: large subjects, throughput

**What.** Criterion 2, large-subject shape: bytes per second of a full
find-all scan of 1 MiB and 16 MiB subjects (OCaml sources, OpenSSH log)
by the top 16 in-subset workload patterns per source, for the six
columns.

**Why.** Few searches over large subjects: the FFI crossing is paid
once per match, not per byte, so this shape isolates engine cost
(including the search optimiser).

## Provenance

As [entry 1](2026-11-19-exp-12-provenance.md). Command:
`EXP=<exp> python3 -I run.py large r1 10 16`, 2026-11-19
17:14-17:27 UTC, load average 1.01-2.01, experiment #11 detected
during part of the series. One process per (engine, source, size,
pattern) and run, `taskset -c 2`, `timeout 120`: load the subject, one
untimed warm-up scan, then one timed scan (scans repeated only until at
least one has been timed: the plan's "one full scan" unit; the 0.5 s
repetition of the short shape was not used here, recorded as a
choice). Find-all: search from the previous match end (one character
further after an empty match). 10 interleaved runs per cell (seed r for
run r). 384 cells, no timeout, no cell with IQR above 10% of its median.
All engines found the same number of matches on every
(subject, pattern), `ocaml-re` included.

## Analysis

- FFI crossing is negligible on the log (few matches): stubs and
  ctypes within 0.5% of C. On the source subjects, where patterns such
  as `,` `(` `;` match every few bytes, the crossing is paid per match:
  stubs lose 5-7%, ctypes about half the throughput.
- Engine cost: the VM runs at a median of 58 MB/s (`ffi`) and 62 MB/s
  (flambda): 57-69 MB/s on single literals, 16-24 MB/s on
  alternations (one choice point per alternative at each byte), against
  medians of 440-660 MB/s for Oniguruma: 7-11x slower. This is mostly "no search optimiser", not "OCaml code":
  on these literal patterns Oniguruma's STR_FAST/MAP skip ahead with
  memchr-like loops while the VM runs its interpreter at each byte.
  Oniguruma's slowest pattern (`\s*(\[)`, 35 MB/s on the log, where the
  `\s*` prefix defeats the skip) is within 2x of the VM's.
- `ocaml-re`'s DFA reaches 290-340 MB/s with no optimiser, which bounds
  what a non-backtracking OCaml matcher can do here.
