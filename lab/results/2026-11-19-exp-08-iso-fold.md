# Experiment #8, entry 6: ISO-8859-1 multi-character case folding

**What.** The one-level fold expansion of `SEMANTICS.md` section 4 (the
items of `get_case_fold_codes_by_str`, expanded per pattern position as
`unravel_case_fold_string` does) in the four tools, run on the 10 cases,
with lemma F checked: `0xDF` matches each of `ss`, `SS`, `sS`, `Ss`
(`Some (0, 2)`) and each of them matches `0xDF` (`Some (0, 1)`).

**Why.** Fourth feature of step 4.

## Automation failures and manual help

- **Lean, computability help** (`lean/Fold.lean:31-45`): `unravel`
  written with a two-byte lookahead (nested `match`, then an
  as-pattern `c :: rest@(d :: rest')`) was compiled by well-founded
  recursion and marked irreducible, and `decide +kernel` failed to
  reduce every case. Rewritten with a pending-byte accumulator
  (`unr`), which is structural. The comparisons in `one` were also
  rewritten with `Nat.ble`/`Nat.beq` while isolating the failure; it
  was not established that this second change is needed.
- **F\*** (`fstar/Fold.fst:44-45`): explicit `(decreases w)` on
  `prefix_at`; the default lexicographic order on its arguments fails.
- **Rocq**, **Verus**: none.

## Analysis

Lemma F is a finite statement and every tool closes it by computation,
so this feature measures the cost of *encoding* the fold table and the
expansion, not of proving; all four are short. A stronger lemma over a
symbolic subject (`0xDF` matches at `i` whenever `s[i..i+2)` is a
spelling of `ss`) is a follow-up. F\*'s 3.4 s is the slowest check of
the experiment, spent in `assert_norm` on the cases. Not covered:
character classes (`[\xDF]`, `apply_all_case_fold`), other encodings,
the case-fold flags (`IGNORECASE_IS_ASCII`).
