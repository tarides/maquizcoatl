# exp-05 runB: read accessor, Isabelle-LLVM composition, Miri, Aeneas warning, F\* master

## What

Run B of the pipeline's take-back of experiment #5 (issue #13; plan
section "Taken back by the pipeline (2026-11-21)"): the two maintainer
decisions on the milestone 2 questions and the scheduled follow-ups.

1. **Isabelle-LLVM, shape (a):** `vu_fold_all`'s hypothesis on the
   client is now derived, in a proof, from a client contract on the
   function it is handed and from `vu_i_apply_correct`.
2. **Read accessor, decision (b):** `proto.h` gets
   `int vu_get_subject_byte(const vu_args *args, int i)`, which returns
   `args->subject[i]` (0 to 255) when `0 <= i < args->subject_len`,
   `VU_ERR_INVALID_ARGUMENT` otherwise without reading. The C caller
   calls it from a callout (storing what it reads with `vu_set_slot`),
   with a subject length shorter than the buffer, and directly. All six
   prototypes implement it; each gets a second negative control
   (`run.sh -r`) that removes the upper bound test.
3. **Miri test of shape (b)** for Verus and Aeneas.
4. **Aeneas warning** "extracted external, unknown definitions":
   explained; the generated model `Vu.lean` committed.
5. **Issue #29:** F\* master built from source, `pulse/probes/eta.sh`
   rerun against it.

Raw outputs (redacted by `lib.sh`, checked for host, user and home
names before commit) are in
[`2026-11-21-exp-05-runB-raw/`](2026-11-21-exp-05-runB-raw/).

## Why

The milestone 1-2 review left two needs-decision points
([review](https://github.com/tarides/maquizcoatl/issues/13#issuecomment-7038827493),
[decisions](https://github.com/tarides/maquizcoatl/issues/13#issuecomment-7059928154)).
The prototype read no engine state through an accessor, so the
ownership a tool lends for a *read* during a callout (the case of
Oniguruma's callouts and `onig_get_*_by_callout_args`) was untested:
Verus, Aeneas and Isabelle-LLVM lend full ownership only. Milestones 3
and 4 are to use the extended prototype. The Miri test settles the
Rust aliasing question that the `prototypes-m2` entry left open, and
the Aeneas warning was unexplained.

Every normal run links with `caller/caller.c` and prints exactly the
new `caller/expected.txt` (four new lines: the callout reading the
subject with lengths 3 and 2, the direct reads of `0xff 0x80 0x00` and
the empty subject); the reference does too. Every control is rejected
by the prover (`build_status=3`).

How each tool expresses shared read access to the subject, and what it
costs (details in `table.md`, notes):

- **Why3** (+12 trusted lines): the subject is an immutable field
  (`csubject_len`, `csubject`) of the read-only model `const_args`, with
  an invariant (length non-negative, bytes in 0-255), read by two
  driver entries. Why3 has no ownership: nothing in the model says that
  the callout may not write the subject.
- **F\* + Pulse** (+3): a fraction `#ps` of the subject array
  (`A.pts_to v.subject #ps s`), lent to the callout contract and to the
  accessor and given back with the same bytes. The field `subject`
  becomes a Pulse array, which KaRaMeL still emits as `uint8_t *` (the
  layout check passes). New precondition on the accessor:
  `SZ.fits_u32`, since Pulse indexes arrays with `size_t` and F\*
  leaves its width to the target; it is a fact about the platform that
  the C caller is assumed to meet.
- **Verus** (+16) and **Charon + Aeneas** (+16): neither can read
  through the raw pointer `subject` (Verus would need a tracked
  permission that the C caller cannot provide; Aeneas models
  `ConstRawPtr` as opaque). The trusted glue turns it into a shared
  slice of `subject_len` bytes and the verified accessor takes the
  slice; Verus states the length agreement as a precondition, Aeneas
  as a theorem hypothesis. Neither prototype lends anything for the
  subject across the callout: the verified side holds no permission on
  it, so the read stays outside the ownership model.
- **VST + CompCert** (+10): a readable share of the subject array
  (`data_at shs (tarray tuchar n)`), lent to the callout funspec and to
  the accessor; a read share cannot write, so the bytes come back the
  same by construction.
- **Isabelle-LLVM** (+6 for the accessor, +2 for the composition): no
  fractional permissions; the callout hypothesis and the accessor take
  the subject (`ll_range` of `subject_len` bytes) whole, and the
  contract says the same bytes come back. **It is the one tool of the
  six that cannot express the read without lending full ownership.**

### Isabelle-LLVM, shape (a)

New in `VU.thy`: `respects_i_apply g rc` (the client reaches the record
only through the `vu_i_apply` it is given: it preserves every property
of the record that each call of `vu_i_apply` preserves),
`vu_i_apply_rec_wf` (from `vu_i_apply_correct`, by `cons_post_rule`)
and `vu_fold_all_composed`: under `respects_i_apply g rc`, `vu_fold_all
g rc` returns the record well formed. The hypothesis of
`vu_fold_all_correct` is derived rather than assumed, so `TRUSTED` now
lists `respects_i_apply` (8 lines) instead of that hypothesis and the
definition of "well formed" (6 lines). This puts Isabelle-LLVM level
with Aeneas's `Respects`, except that no model of `caller.c`'s client
is shown to meet the contract (Aeneas has `tableClient_respects`).

### Miri (`miri.log`, `miri-first.log`)

`miri/client.rs` is a pure-Rust client: during callouts in each
direction, made while the verified `callout_step` holds a `&mut VuArgs`,
it calls `vu_get_slot`, `vu_set_slot` and `vu_get_subject_byte`
through the raw pointer it was given; it reads the subject with a
length shorter than the buffer; and its apply-all function calls
`vu_i_apply` back through the pointer `vu_fold_all` hands it.
`miri/miri.sh` builds it against the Aeneas sources as they are and
against the Verus sources erased to plain Rust by
`miri/erase_verus.py` (Verus's own erasure needs its rustc; the script
removes the `verus!` wrapper, `#[verifier]` attributes, spec functions,
`requires`/`ensures` clauses and ghost statements, and keeps the
executable statements, `shim.rs` and `ffi.rs` unchanged).

- First run (`miri-first.log`, sources of the development copy before
  the fix): both prototypes pass under Tree Borrows, and both fail under
  Stacked Borrows in the new glue `subject_of`: it made the slice from
  `args.subject.as_ref()`, a reference to one byte, and the slice reads
  three. A bug in trusted code written in this run, which only Stacked
  Borrows caught.
- After the fix (the slice is made from the raw pointer, after a NULL
  check), the recorded run passes for both prototypes under Stacked
  Borrows and Tree Borrows. In particular, the client's accesses
  through the raw pointer while the verified side holds `&mut VuArgs`
  are accepted by both models: the pointer is derived from that
  reference and the verified side does not use it during the call.

What Miri does not cover: Verus's compiled code is not the erased copy
(only the trusted `shim.rs` and `ffi.rs` are byte-identical); and Miri
checks this execution only.

### Aeneas warning

The warning refers to `core::num::<impl i32>::MAX`, read by `i_apply`,
which Aeneas maps to `core.num.I32.MAX` of its Lean library. In
`export_global` (Aeneas `src/Translate.ml:1005-1006` at `8f4466db`) the
flag behind the warning is set for any global without a body, before
the check for a library definition, so the warning is a false positive
here. Checks on the bench host: a one-function crate reading
`i32::MAX` gets the warning, while crates with a required trait
method, a constant, a raw pointer, a slice or a module excluded with
`--exclude` do not; `aeneas -split-files` on our LLBC writes `Types.lean`
and `Funs.lean` and no file of external definitions. The generated
model is committed as
[`runB-raw/aeneas/Vu.lean`](2026-11-21-exp-05-runB-raw/aeneas/Vu.lean)
(354 lines, sha256 `64c6bedc...`, as in `build.log`); it has no axiom
or opaque definition. The "callee is a pure function of its arguments"
assumption was already in Aeneas's callee-assumptions column of
`table.md` (added in `1dcc3dc`); it is kept.

## Verdict

All six prototypes implement the read accessor, are proven, link with
the C caller and print exactly the new `expected.txt`; every clamp and
every read control is rejected. Verdicts are unchanged: Pulse and VST
"works", the other four "works with trusted shim". Trusted lines: Why3
140, Pulse 28, Verus 118, Aeneas 125, VST 54, Isabelle-LLVM 108.
Pulse and VST express the read with a read-only share; Verus and Aeneas
only through a shared slice made in trusted glue, outside their
ownership model; Why3 has no ownership; Isabelle-LLVM lends the subject
whole. Isabelle-LLVM's shape (a) is now proved under a client contract,
composed with `vu_i_apply_correct`. Miri accepts the Rust client's
re-entrant accesses for Verus and Aeneas, after it caught a bug in the
new glue. The F\* eta-reduction bug is still on master.
